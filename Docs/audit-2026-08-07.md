# Audit of what changed on 7 August 2026

The previous audit read the project as it stood before this day's work. Since
then the timeline gained bounds and a reload policy, countdowns moved onto
dynamic dates, the age caption became a capture moment, rows changed shape, a
state for "no limits arrived" appeared, and the bar lost its `GeometryReader`.
Every one of those carries claims about WidgetKit that nothing had checked.

Two axes, as before. Three outcomes: confirmed, contradicted, not found.
Nothing here is fixed; this is a list.

---

## Axis 1 — claims about the platform

### Confirmed

**Dynamic dates keep counting while the extension is not running.**
`apple/widgetkit-dynamic-dates.md`: "Show up-to-date, time-based information in
your widget even when it isn't running", and views that "continue updating
while your widget is visible". This is what the countdown now rests on.

**Entries no closer than about five minutes.**
`apple/widgetkit-keeping-up-to-date.md`: "Your timeline provider should create
timeline entries that are at least about 5 minutes apart." Held by
`TimelineTests`.

**`contentMarginsDisabled()` then `padding(_:)` is the documented order.**
`apple/widgetkit-creating-views.md` gives exactly those two steps, in that
order, and that is what `CCWidgetBundle` does.

**`.after(_:)` is legitimate for content that changes unpredictably.**
Same file: "a provider can specify a different date altogether if the timeline
might change before or after reaching the end of the entries."

### Contradicted

**The horizon and the 30-minute ceiling go against a documented
recommendation.** Apple asks for the opposite: "generate a timeline for as many
future dates as possible. Keep the interval of entries in the timeline as large
as possible for the content you display."

We now cap the timeline at two hours and the gap between entries at thirty
minutes. The reason is sound — our content is written from outside by a process
we do not control, so no timeline can predict it, and the last entry is the
only thing that brings the provider back when the app's window is closed. But
it *is* a departure from a documented recommendation, and it is not recorded as
one. `SPEC` 2.4 explains the mechanism and does not say that Apple advises
otherwise.

**Severity: worth a paragraph in SPEC, not a change of behaviour.** The
practice is defensible; the silence about the recommendation is not.

### Not found in the sources

**That dynamic date styles read the system clock and ignore the date the entry
carries.** Measured here on 7 August (a probe printed "2 г. 8 мес" for an entry
stamped in 2023), and everything about testing them rests on it. Apple does not
state it anywhere in the cached pages.

**That WidgetKit archives a widget's states with size proposals a renderer does
not make.** This is the mechanism behind the crash — `_ArchivedViewHost.
archiveStates` in the stack, and eighty-one clean `ImageRenderer` runs against
a tile that crashed every time. Nothing in the documentation describes it.

**That a crashing extension leaves the last successful frame on screen.**
Observed, and it is the reason two sessions looked in the wrong place: the tile
showed old data and old layout instead of going blank or showing a placeholder.
Not documented anywhere I could find.

### Newly found — a claim of ours that is *less* true than it was

**`widgetContentMargins` exists.** `apple/widgetkit-creating-views.md`: "To
access the system's default content margins for an environment, use the
`widgetContentMargins` environment variable."

`SPEC` 9 records a departure that says Apple publishes no margin values for
macOS and ours were chosen by measurement. That is true of the published
tables and false of the runtime: the system will hand us its own margins if
asked. The departure could be replaced by reading the value.

### A contradiction between two Apple sources

**What rendering mode a system widget gets on Mac.**

- `apple/widgetkit-strategy.md`, in the section about `WidgetFamily.systemSmall`:
  "On Mac, it uses the **accented** rendering mode."
- HIG *Widgets*, the rendering-mode table (read live, 6 August): for Mac,
  Full-color is "Desktop and Notification Center", **Accented is "Not
  supported"**.

These cannot both be right for macOS 26. It matters here: in accented mode the
system splits the view into an accent group and a primary group and paints each
a single colour, which would erase the green / yellow / red levels the gauges
use. Section 8 leans on those colours, and `Thresholds` names them.

**Not measured.** The project reads `\.widgetFamily` and neither
`\.widgetRenderingMode` nor `\.widgetContentMargins`, so it cannot currently
tell which mode it is in. A one-line log from the view would answer it. Until
then, treat "our colours reach the desktop" as unverified.

Mitigating: every level also carries a glyph, so meaning does not rest on
colour alone — which is what HIG asks for anyway.

## Axis 2 — decisions about how the thing is built

### Confirmed

**Showing when the snapshot was taken.** HIG *Widgets*: "If people are likely to
check your widget more frequently than you can update it, consider displaying
text that describes when the data was last updated." "updated at 11:50" is
exactly that.

**Explaining an absence instead of showing dashes.** HIG *Widgets* asks for a
message when a widget cannot show its content — the example given is "Sign in to
view reservations". The "Limits have not arrived" panel is the same shape of
answer.

**A compact line on the medium tile.** HIG *Widgets*: "Balance information
density. Sparse layouts can make the widget seem unnecessary, while overly
dense layouts are less glanceable." Dropping the reset moment from the medium
row and keeping it on the large one is that trade, made deliberately.

### Not found in the sources

**A minimum width for the bar, with the caption shrinking instead.** No source
speaks to it. Ours, and the number (40 pt) is a judgement named as such in
`TextMetricsTests`.

**Drawing the bar with `scaleEffect` rather than reading geometry.** Forced by
the crash, not by guidance. Worth knowing: it changes the shape of the fill's
trailing edge slightly at low percentages, visible on close inspection of the
context row at 94 %.

**The two-hour horizon as a freshness mechanism.** The bound itself contradicts
a recommendation (above); using it to keep data fresh is our own idea and is
not discussed anywhere.

---

## What this leaves for the owner

1. **Decide whether to record the horizon as a departure** in `SPEC` 2.4, with
   the quotation that it departs from. My reading: yes, it is a departure and
   should say so.
2. **Decide whether to read `widgetContentMargins`** instead of the hand-picked
   14 points. It would remove one departure and one measured constant.
3. **Measure the rendering mode on this Mac** before trusting the level
   colours. One log line answers the contradiction between Apple's own sources.
